# Why I Converted to Eastern Orthodox Christianity

Hello. My name is Milton McDonald. I am an Orthodox Christian, a husband and father, and a software engineer. My family and I have recently gone through a huge transition in our lives, converting from evangelicalism to Eastern Orthodox Christianity. I wrote a memoir detailing that journey and have turned it into this webpage.

I grew up in an evangelical Christian home within the Christian and Missionary Alliance tradition. My love for Jesus was sincere from an early age. I said the sinner's prayer, was baptized, attended Bible camp, sang hymns, participated in youth group, and genuinely desired to follow Christ. The Christian faith was not merely something my family believed. It was the world in which I was raised.

Like many evangelicals of my generation, my spiritual upbringing was shaped by revivalism, dispensational theology, and a strong emphasis on the end times. Conversations about the Rapture, the Antichrist, and biblical prophecy were common. Alongside these ideas were warnings about various dangers to the faith: secular culture, certain forms of entertainment, Satanism, and even Roman Catholicism. Legalism also played a significant role in shaping my spiritual outlook. Christians didn't drink alcohol. Certain movies, music, and activities were viewed with suspicion. Purity culture taught us to fear moral failure and to constantly examine ourselves for signs of spiritual compromise.

Throughout my teenage years, I found myself trapped in a familiar cycle. I would attend a retreat, convention, or revival event, experience a powerful emotional response, rededicate my life to Christ, and return home determined to change. After a season of spiritual enthusiasm, however, discouragement would inevitably follow. Then the cycle would begin again. I loved God, but I could never seem to find the lasting spiritual transformation I was seeking.

As I grew older, I began to feel increasingly dissatisfied with modern church culture. The casual atmosphere, entertainment-driven worship, and ever-changing trends left me unsettled. My criticism was not directed at any one congregation. Rather, I found myself asking a deeper question: What is the right way to worship God? If God is holy, should worship not reflect that holiness? I longed for reverence, beauty, mystery, and a sense of connection to something far greater than myself.

During my college years, many of the assumptions I had inherited came under scrutiny. I entered a period of deconstruction in which I questioned nearly everything I believed. For a time, I came dangerously close to abandoning Christianity altogether. Yet it was during this period that I encountered some of the greatest minds in Christian history through the Great Books of the Western World. Among them, the writings of Saint Augustine had a profound effect on me. Through these works I discovered an intellectual and spiritual depth within Christianity that I had never fully appreciated before. Rather than losing my faith, I began to make it my own.

In the years that followed, I became deeply involved in church life. I attended men's retreats, participated in Bible studies, volunteered in ministry, and eventually served as a youth leader. Yet despite my activity, something still felt incomplete. I knew a great deal about Christianity, but I lacked a meaningful prayer life. My spiritual growth seemed stagnant. I often felt as though I was spinning my wheels, moving constantly but making little progress.

At the same time, the Christian and Missionary Alliance was increasingly influenced by charismatic theology and practices. Concepts such as faith healing, speaking in tongues, soul care ministries, and other spiritual experiences became more common. While I do not doubt the sincerity of many involved in these movements, I became increasingly concerned by what appeared to be a lack of theological boundaries and spiritual guardrails. These concerns became personal after a particularly troubling charismatic experience affected our youth group. Watching young people navigate powerful spiritual claims without clear guidance raised difficult questions that I could no longer ignore.

My search for answers eventually led me to the ancient Christian faith. As I began studying the history of the Church, the writings of the Church Fathers, and the beliefs of the earliest Christians, I encountered Eastern Orthodoxy. What began as curiosity gradually became a years-long investigation.

I resisted many of Orthodoxy's teachings at first. The theological issues were not small. Questions of authority, salvation, Scripture, tradition, the sacraments, and the communion of saints required serious study and prayer. There were moments when I wanted Orthodoxy to be wrong. Yet the deeper I dug, the more I found that the Orthodox Church possessed the historical continuity, theological coherence, and spiritual depth I had been searching for my entire life.

Eventually, after years of study and prayer, my family and I visited an Orthodox parish. What I encountered there was not merely a different style of Christianity. It felt like Christianity in its fullness: reverent worship, sacramental life, historic continuity, and a living connection to the faith of the apostles and the saints.

The journey was not without cost. My transition ultimately resulted in the loss of leadership positions that had been important parts of my life and ministry. Yet even amid the difficulties, I experienced a deep sense of peace and conviction that God was leading us where we needed to be.

In time, my entire family became catechumens. On the Feast of Pentecost in 2026, we were received into the Eastern Orthodox Church.

This website is the story of that journey.

I do not share it to attack evangelicalism or to diminish the many people who helped shape my faith. I remain deeply grateful for those who pointed me toward Christ and taught me to love the Scriptures. Rather, I write for those who find themselves asking the same questions that once kept me awake at night. If you are wrestling with the nature of the Church, the meaning of worship, the Christian life, or the faith of the early Christians, perhaps my story will be of some help to you.

Most importantly, I pray that this testimony points not to me, but to the faithfulness of God, who patiently guided me through doubt, struggle, and discovery.

Glory to God for all things.
