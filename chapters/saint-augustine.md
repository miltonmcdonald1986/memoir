Reading Augustine's Confessions marked the end of atheism in my life and the rediscovery of God in my inmost being.

As I read his story, I saw myself. Like me, he had a loving and praying mother. Like me, he fell into intellectual doubt and pride, as well as bad behavior and a life devoted to pleasure. He wandered, resisted God, explored the heretical cult of the Manicheans, and failed to live up to who he was meant to be. He even boarded a ship and fled to Rome to start a new life.

But his mother would not give up on him. She followed him and continued to weep and pray for her son. When she visited the Bishop of Milan to share her grief and fears, he reassured her: "It cannot be that the son of these tears should perish." And he was right.

Augustine eventually met his match in Bishop Ambrose, a man who could answer his intellectual objections and prove that educated people could be Christians. In time, Augustine came to accept the truth of Christianity, but he was unwilling to give up the life he was living. He was torn between a desire for God and his attachments to sin, pleasure, ambition, and passion. He famously prayed, "Grant me chastity and continence, but not yet."

But he could not maintain this divided life. A man cannot serve two masters. In a moment of deep emotional and spiritual turmoil, he heard a child's voice repeatedly crying out, "Take up and read. Take up and read." Believing that God was speaking to him through the child, Augustine picked up a copy of Paul's epistles and opened it to a random passage:

"Not in rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness, not in strife and envying. But put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh, to fulfil the lusts thereof."

As soon as he had read it, all doubt vanished from his mind. He resolved at once to abandon his old life and commit himself fully to Christ.

When I read this, I broke. I collapsed onto the floor of my bedroom and wept like never before. As I read about Augustine rushing to his mother to tell her the good news, I knew that the same thing was happening to me. I had chosen Christ again, truly, freely, and without reservation. I was never going back. Sharing this with my wife was deeply healing for both of us. I will always be thankful to God for bringing the Great Books, and especially Augustine's confessions, into my life.

Glory to God!
